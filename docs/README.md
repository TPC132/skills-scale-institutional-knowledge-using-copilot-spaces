# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This README provides a central entry point to OctoAcme's project management process documents and a quick summary of the team's operating model.

## Summary of project management processes

OctoAcme follows a structured, customer-first model that moves from project initiation through planning, execution, release, and retrospective. New ideas are validated by confirming the business need, identifying stakeholders, defining success metrics, and creating a lightweight one-pager with scope, timeline, risks, and resource needs. Once a project is approved, the team turns the initiative into a prioritized backlog with clear acceptance criteria, milestones, and a definition of done to keep work concrete and measurable.

The project model relies on clear ownership across core roles. Project Managers coordinate delivery, communication, milestones, and risk management; Product Managers define outcomes and priorities; developers build and test the solution; QA validates quality and acceptance criteria; and stakeholders provide input, approvals, and business context. This mix of ownership and collaboration creates a shared operating rhythm and helps the team stay aligned across cross-functional work.

Communication is built into the team's cadence through daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, milestone reviews, and stakeholder updates. The team tracks blockers and dependencies in a risk register and escalates issues through a defined path from the delivery team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor when needed. This keeps problems visible, reduces ambiguity, and ensures important decisions happen with the right level of context.

Quality assurance is treated as a continuous delivery practice rather than a final step. OctoAcme emphasizes unit, integration, and smoke tests where applicable, along with CI checks, security scanning, and manual QA when a feature needs acceptance validation. PRs are expected to be small, include issue links and acceptance criteria, and pass automated checks before merge. Retrospectives and continuous improvement loops close the process by capturing lessons, assigning owners, and turning insights into actionable improvements.
